> done when
>     1. The score order is: no overlap, then fewest crossings, then no backward wires, then wire length.
>     2. The rules are:
>        - Nodes in one exec chain share a row.
>        - A branch or a loop body steps one row down.
>        - A loop body continues right on the same row, and "Completed" steps one row down.
>        - A getter sits left of its consumer, level with the pin it feeds, with a gap.
>        - Getters for one consumer are stacked in pin order.
>        - A pure chain moves as one group, below-left of its consumer, flowing left to right.
>     3. The fixer does not refuse a space problem that it can solve.
>
> Verify
>     Lay out a 30-node function with a loop and 10 getters. The result has 0 overlaps, 0 crossings and straight exec rows.
>
> Why the score is an order and not a sum
>     A weighted total would let a large crossing count outweigh an overlap. An overlap is the one outcome that makes a graph unreadable, so the list puts it first.
>
> The relation to T18
>     The six rules are T18's siblings. T18 asks that the report and the fixer flag a getter by the same criterion. This card is the criterion for everything else. Two tools that disagree about one property is the shape that T06 and T07 also carry.
